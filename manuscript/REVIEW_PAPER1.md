# Referee Report — Paper 1 (HBMAE Methodology)

Manuscript: *Hierarchical Bayesian Mechanism-Adequacy Estimation for Molten Salt Radiolysis Networks: A Practical Framework with Validation Against the Pulse-Radiolysis Literature*
Target journal: *SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification* (SIAM/ASA JUQ)
File reviewed: `/Users/tanome/projects/MoltenSaltRadiolysis/manuscript/article_paper1.tex` (2584 lines)

## Recommendation

**Major revisions.** The methodological synthesis is well-motivated, the theorems are sensibly chosen, and the application is genuinely difficult. But several theorems are stated under regularity conditions that fail or are unverified in the actual application (notably the i.i.d. assumption in Theorem 2, the bounded-discrepancy assumption in Theorem 3, and the per-modality "unbiased at the truth" assumption in Theorem 4); the constants in the Theorem-3 bound are not the constants the paper claims; the MCMC chain that is the central empirical product reports no numerical convergence diagnostics; and the comparator study is under-specified for the JUQ standard of reproducibility. None of these is fatal — but the manuscript is not currently of the rigor that JUQ readers expect.

## Summary (≈ 200 words)

The paper introduces HBMAE, a Bayesian framework for chemical-kinetic-network calibration that composes (i) a constrained-topology spike-and-slab prior, (ii) a hierarchical Arrhenius layer across salt hosts, (iii) a Godambe-balanced composite likelihood across four observation modalities, (iv) a low-dimensional parametric Kennedy–O'Hagan discrepancy, and (v) an additive facility-effect hierarchy with explicit gauge fixing. Six theorems establish RJ-MCMC ergodicity on a constrained topology graph, a cross-host Bernstein–von Mises theorem with a host-heterogeneity floor, finite-sample discrepancy-bias bounds, composite-estimator sandwich asymptotics, a censored Bayes-factor lower bound, and gauge identifiability of the facility hierarchy. Empirical validation runs on Iwamatsu–Horne Cr and Zn pulse-radiolysis data and the Phillips 2022 NaCl–UCl₃ NULL benchmark, plus a synthetic-data comparison against four competitors (Galagali–Marzouk RJMCMC, Kennedy–O'Hagan with GP discrepancy, B2BDC, PSIS-LOO). The composition is the principal novelty; the individual layers are mostly classical, and the manuscript is honest about that. The application is well-chosen; the theorems are reasonable, but several have undetected gaps in their stated regularity conditions, and the empirical validation is presented at lower numerical resolution than a JUQ submission requires.

## Major Concerns

1. **Theorem 2 (Bernstein–von Mises) assumes i.i.d. per-host observations, which is false for the actual application.** Assumption A2.1 requires the per-host data `D^(s) = {x_{s,1}, ..., x_{s,n_s}}` to be i.i.d. from a parametric family `p_{θ^(s)}`. In the paper's own application, the per-host data are stiff ODE trajectories (dense correlated time-series, modality M1), scalar Arrhenius pairs derived from a multi-temperature fit (modality M4), and censored constraints (modality M3). These are not i.i.d. samples from a common parametric likelihood; they require either a martingale or empirical-process BvM, *or* a misspecified-BvM in the Kleijn–van der Vaart sense. As stated, Theorem 2 does not apply to the integrated 24-parameter MCMC of §9. **Fix:** either (a) restate the theorem under correlated-data conditions citing Kleijn & van der Vaart (2012) and Castillo & Nickl (2014); or (b) explicitly limit the theorem's claim to the M2 modality (scalar rates) which *is* approximately i.i.d., and add an honest remark that the M1/M3 modalities are covered only heuristically.

2. **Theorem 3 (parametric-discrepancy bias) has a constant-tracking problem and an unverified boundedness assumption.** The headline bound (eq. 1433) is written as `‖θ̂ − θ*‖ ≤ C₁(n) B + C₂(n) ‖μ_prior − θ*‖`, with `C₁(n) := nL_ζ/(σ²λ_min(A_n))`. In the prior-dominated regime the manuscript then claims `λ_min(A_n) ≍ σ_prior^{-2}`, which gives a *first* coefficient that scales as `n L_ζ σ_prior²/σ²` — i.e. growing linearly in n. This is the opposite of what one wants from a regularization bound: in the prior-dominated regime, the bias should be *bounded by the prior strength*, not grow with n. The proof in §A.3 confirms this scaling and writes "the stochastic remainder is `O_P(n^{-1/2})` in both regimes" — but the deterministic discrepancy-bias term is `O(nB)` in the same regime, which is not what the verbal description claims. The reader must check that the regime condition `n σ_prior² λ₀ / σ² ≤ 1` is consistent with the rest of the argument: if `σ_prior² → ∞` (informative prior weak), `λ_min(A_n) ≍ n`, the first term is `O(B)`. If `σ_prior²` is small, the regime applies but the bound now scales with `n`, which is meaningless as an absolute bound. The theorem as stated does not deliver "finite-sample regularization." **Fix:** rewrite the bound with a *single* explicit constant matrix `A_n^{-1}` and characterize when the operator-norm of `A_n^{-1}(n L_ζ B / σ²)` is small — namely, when `σ_prior² ≪ σ² / (n L_ζ²)`. Then the interpretation paragraph after the theorem needs to be rewritten to match the actual bound.

   Assumption A3.3 also requires the discrepancy to be uniformly bounded `|δ(x;ω)| ≤ B` on the prior support. Equation (4.13) in §4.4 says the prior is `ω ∼ N(0, diag(τ_j²))` chosen so `|δ| ≤ B` with prior probability 0.99. **0.99 is not "for all ω in the prior support."** With a Gaussian prior, every realization has positive density and there is no compact prior support. The discrepancy bias derivation needs to be redone with `|δ| ≤ B` holding in expectation or in probability, or the prior on ω must be truncated.

3. **The integrated 24-parameter MCMC reports no numerical convergence diagnostics.** §11 of the appendix says only "convergence diagnostics (R̂, integrated autocorrelation time, effective sample size) were within acceptable thresholds for each parameter." For a JUQ submission with 24 parameters, 56 walkers, 200 production steps, and the central claim that posterior medians recover literature values, the reader needs the actual numbers: per-parameter R̂ values, per-parameter ESS, the integrated autocorrelation time τ (so the effective independent-sample count can be computed: 56 × 200 / τ), and the autocorrelation traceplots. 200 production steps with affine-invariant ensemble walkers is *very short*; if τ ≳ 50 (entirely plausible for a stiff-ODE posterior with strongly correlated `(log A, E_a)` parameters), the ESS is 56 × 200 / 50 ≈ 224 — too small for credible 95% intervals. **Fix:** add a table of per-parameter R̂ and ESS (Vehtari et al. 2021's rank-normalized split-R̂ is now standard), and report τ explicitly. If the ESS is too small, rerun with more steps; if it is adequate, say so quantitatively.

## Specific Comments (line-by-line, most impactful issues)

- **Line 109 (abstract):** the abstract reports the censored Bayes factor as `ln BF = 316.9 (log₁₀ BF = 137.6)`. Check the conversion: `316.9 / ln(10) = 316.9 / 2.302585 = 137.6`. OK. But report the conversion only once (either ln or log₁₀, not both, in the abstract) and state the inputs (μ_γ₀ − c)/σ_C that produced this number. Without those inputs the number is a black box.

- **Line 119–120 (abstract):** "demonstrate that HBMAE is the unique method that simultaneously recovers ground-truth parameters, handles censored modalities, identifies facility offsets, and produces calibrated uncertainty." "Unique" is too strong — you have compared with four methods on one test problem. Soften to "is the only one of the four comparator methods that..."

- **Line 195:** "the Pikaev microsecond pulse radiolysis of the 1980s and the Iwamatsu picosecond LEAF data of the 2020s disagree by an order of magnitude" — for Zn at 850°C the disagreement is actually ~2 orders (line 1806). Fix the abstract claim to match the body.

- **Line 207–208:** "Existing spike-and-slab Bayesian network-inference frameworks (Galagali–Marzouk) place positive prior mass on the infeasible region and pay a corresponding mixing penalty." A specific number (the 38.3% feasibility rate is in line 791) would be much more compelling at this point. As written this is a generic complaint.

- **§3.1 (line 614–633), Galagali–Marzouk characterization:** the claim that GM "leaves the absence of a hierarchical layer to couple shared reactions across hosts" needs to acknowledge that GM 2019 has a network-topology extension that can in principle support cross-host pooling with a custom kernel; the way it's written makes GM sound categorically inferior, which is unfair.

- **§4.3 (eq. 4.7), Godambe weights:** the weights `w_m = (tr(I_m) + ε_I)^{-1} / Σ_{m'} (tr(I_{m'}) + ε_I)^{-1}` are stated but `I_m` is the *Fisher information at the truth*, which is unknown. In practice you must use the observed information or a plug-in estimate. **Fix:** state explicitly that `Î_m` is the observed information at the posterior mean (or MLE), and that the resulting weights are themselves random; either argue why the randomness is negligible (Slutsky) or update the Godambe sandwich asymptotic theorem (Theorem 4) to account for random weights — the sandwich becomes a different expression involving covariances of the weights with the scores.

- **Eq. (4.7) and Theorem 4 inconsistency:** the glossary (line 2541) defines Godambe information as `J^{-1}KJ^{-1}` while Theorem 4 writes `G = H J^{-1} H`. The standard usage in Lindsay 1988 and Varin–Reid–Firth 2011 is `G_n = H_n J_n^{-1} H_n` (the "godambe" or "sandwich" form), with covariance `H^{-1} J H^{-1}`. The glossary is wrong (it uses both J and K and inverts the structure). Make them consistent.

- **§4.5 (line 884–902), facility-effect prior:** the prior on `b^{(p)}` is `N(0, Σ_b)` with `σ_b = log(10)/2 ≈ 1.15`. Why a Gaussian and not a Student-t (more robust to gross facility miscalibration)? Why a single shared `Σ_b` across all papers, when one might want a heavier-tailed prior for known-problematic facilities (Pikaev 1980s) and tighter prior for verified-recent facilities (Iwamatsu LEAF)? Justify or generalize.

- **§4.7 (lines 911–923), unified posterior:** the joint posterior product writes `∏_s ∏_{i ∈ γ} N(η_i^{(s)} | 0, Λ_i)` — but `γ` is a binary vector, not an index set. Use `i: γ_i = 1`. Minor typo.

- **§4.8 Algorithm 1, line 949–974:** the per-iteration cost paragraph in lines 1082–1102 is good. But the algorithm itself does not have a step to update `Λ_i` (the host-heterogeneity covariance) or `Σ_b` (the facility prior) — are these fixed hyperparameters, or are they being inferred via empirical-Bayes? If fixed, say so explicitly (and that's a non-trivial limitation: a fully Bayesian sampler would put hyperpriors on Λ_i and Σ_b). If inferred, add the steps to the algorithm.

- **§5 Theorem 1, line 1188–1214:** "Aperiodicity. Because the MH acceptance probability α<1 on a set of positive π-measure (the proposal is not exactly π), there is positive probability of remaining at the current state." Technically the chain is aperiodic *if* `α < 1` on a set of positive `π`-measure; you also need to confirm that the within-model emcee stretch is itself reversible with strictly positive probability of staying, which is implicit but should be stated. (Goodman–Weare 2010 has this as Prop. 1; cite it.)

- **§5 Theorem 2 proof, Step 2 (lines 1330–1342):** "Convolving the Gaussian posterior with this Gaussian conditional, p(θ_i | D^(s)) → N(θ̂^(s), Λ_i + n_s^{-1} I_s^{-1})." This is the *prior* convolution, not the *posterior* on θ_i given D^(s). The marginal posterior on θ_i given D^(s) is obtained by *integrating out* θ^(s) against the posterior of θ^(s)|D^(s). The math is right but the verbal description "convolving" obscures it; rewrite as "the marginal posterior on θ_i given D^(s) is obtained by integrating out θ^(s) against the per-host Gaussian posterior (Step 1) and the Gaussian conditional θ^(s)|θ_i ~ N(θ_i, Λ_i); the integral of a Gaussian against a Gaussian gives a Gaussian with variance Λ_i + n_s^{-1} I_s^{-1}."

- **§5 Theorem 2, contraction-regime statement:** the host-heterogeneity-dominated rate is `O(K^{-1})`, *not* `K^{+1}` as the rubric suggests. The manuscript is correct here (line 1311: `Σ̂_i ≈ (K Λ_i^{-1} + π_0)^{-1} = O(K^{-1})`). However, the empirical verification in §9.5 (line 2017–2028) reports linear-regression exponents α = 0.92 on log A and α = 1.13 on E_a, "bracketing the host-heterogeneity-dominated prediction α = +1." Here `α` is the exponent of *precision* not variance, so α = +1 corresponds to variance scaling `K^{-1}`. **Add one sentence clarifying:** "precision = 1/variance; α = +1 in precision corresponds to variance scaling as K^{-1}, consistent with the host-heterogeneity floor of (4.27)." Otherwise readers will think there's an inconsistency.

- **Theorem 3 (line 1412–1455), prior assumption:** A3.5 sets a Gaussian prior `N(μ_prior, σ_prior² I)`. The bound (4.13) then uses `λ_min(A_n)`. But `σ_prior²` is a scalar and `I` is the identity — so the prior precision matrix is `σ_prior^{-2} I`. The prior is therefore isotropic, which is a very strong assumption that does not match the prior used in §4.5 (different scales for `log A` and `E_a`). **Fix:** generalize to `θ ~ N(μ_prior, Σ_prior)` and replace `σ_prior^{-2}` with `λ_min(Σ_prior^{-1})` throughout, or be explicit that this theorem is for the isotropic-prior special case only.

- **Theorem 4 (line 1486–1502), Assumption A4.2:** "Each modality is unbiased at the truth: E[U_m(θ*)] = 0 for all m." For modality M3 (censored observations) the score has a non-trivial structure that does *not* obviously satisfy this without additional regularity (the censored likelihood is not the same parametric family as the uncensored one, and the score at the truth depends on whether the truth lies inside or outside the censoring region). State this explicitly and verify it for the Phillips NULL benchmark (truth predicts non-detection; censoring direction matches; score-zero condition holds).

- **Theorem 5 lower bound (line 1568–1574):** the Mills ratio bound gives `Φ(-k) ≤ φ(k)/k`, so `BF_{10} ≥ (1/2) × k/(φ(k)) = (1/2) × k √(2π) exp(k²/2) = k√(2π/4) exp(k²/2) × ... `. Wait — `(1/2) / Φ(-k) ≥ (1/2) × k/(φ(k))` requires `Φ(-k) ≤ φ(k)/k`, which gives `1/Φ(-k) ≥ k/φ(k)`, hence `(1/2)/Φ(-k) ≥ k/(2φ(k)) = k √(2π) exp(k²/2) / 2`. The paper writes `k√(2π)/2 × exp(k²/2)`. **OK, this is correct**, but the algebra is opaque; spell it out: `(1/2)/Φ(-k) ≥ (k/2)/φ(k) = (k/2) × √(2π) × exp(k²/2) = (k√(2π)/2) × exp(k²/2)`. The proof currently jumps without explanation.

- **§9 (validation), §9.5 (Theorem 2 host-scaling simulation):** add the actual numbers of replicates, the regression statistics (R²), and the prior used. As written it's a one-paragraph summary that's not reproducible.

- **§10 (comparison), Table 9 (M0–M4):** the synthetic-benchmark numbers (line 2108–2113) report `Δ log A`, `Δ E_a`, coverage, and RMSE across 50 replicates. But the standard errors / Monte Carlo error of each entry are not reported. ## Strengths

1. **The problem identification is clear and compelling.** The five-way decomposition of the radiation-chemistry calibration problem (multi-scale, multi-modality, cross-laboratory bias, cross-salt mechanistic transfer, topology constraints) is genuinely the right way to frame this body of work. The intro is the strongest part of the paper.

2. **The synthesis is a real contribution.** Each layer individually is classical (constrained spike-and-slab from Galagali–Marzouk, hierarchical Arrhenius from Gelman-style multilevel modeling, Godambe composite from Lindsay/Varin–Reid, parametric KO from Brynjarsdóttir–O'Hagan, facility-effect gauge from standard random-effect ANOVA), but the integrated five-layer posterior with explicit identifiability and ergodicity proofs is, as far as I am aware, not in the literature.

3. **Theorem 5 (censored Bayes factor) is correct and useful.** The lower-bound expression `(1/2) k √(2π) exp(k²/2)` is a clean asymptotic, and the application to the Phillips NULL is a tidy demonstration of how one-sided benchmarks should enter Bayesian model comparison.

4. **The facility-effect gauge analysis (Theorem 6) is well-motivated and correct.** The three gauge-breaking conditions (reference, centering, calibration) are stated precisely and the proof is clean.

5. **The Pikaev–Iwamatsu Zn quantification (b^(Pikaev) = −3.35 [−4.53, −2.44])** is a concrete result with measurable impact on the molten-salt radiochemistry community.

6. **The synthetic-benchmark design (§10.1) is well-thought-out.** Three salt hosts × two facilities × ground-truth Arrhenius with imposed offsets is exactly the structure that exercises the five layers diagnostically.

7. **The two-flip ergodicity certificate by exhaustive enumeration (Lemma 5.1)** is an honest, rigorous solution to the topology-connectivity problem for the specific kernel — a refreshing contrast to the usual hand-wave.

## Suggested rewrites (5–10 specific paragraphs)

1. **Theorem 2 statement — replace i.i.d. with martingale-difference / correlated-data condition.**
   *Current* (line 1262): "(A2.1) For each host s, the observations D^(s) = {x_{s,1}, ..., x_{s,n_s}} are i.i.d. from a parametric family {p_{θ^(s)} : θ^(s) ∈ Θ} with Θ ⊂ R^d open and convex."
   *Proposed:* "(A2.1) For each host s, the observation process {y_{s,j}} is generated by the parametric forward model y_{s,j} = ζ(x_{s,j}; θ^(s)) + ε_{s,j} with ε_{s,j} ∼ N(0, σ_{s,j}²) independent across j and host-stationary in the sense of (Kleijn–van der Vaart 2012, §3). The per-host log-likelihood is the sum of the n_s observation-wise Gaussian log-densities; the integrated log-likelihood is the cross-host sum. Under correct specification of ζ at θ^{(s),*}, the per-host MLE θ̂^{(s,MLE)} is asymptotically N(θ^{(s),*}, n_s^{-1} I_s^{-1}) by standard regularity (van der Vaart 1998, Theorem 5.39)." This generalises the theorem to the actual application without weakening its conclusion.

2. **Theorem 3 bound — make the regime structure explicit.**
   *Current* (line 1448–1454): "In the finite-sample, prior-dominated regime n σ_prior² λ₀ / σ² ≤ 1, we have λ_min(A_n) ≍ σ_prior^{-2} and (4.13) specializes to ‖θ̂_n − θ*‖ ≤ n L_ζ σ_prior² / σ² × B + ‖μ_prior − θ*‖ + O_P(n^{-1/2})."
   *Proposed:* "In the finite-sample, prior-dominated regime (defined as n σ_prior² λ₀ / σ² ≤ 1, equivalently σ_prior² ≤ σ² / (n λ₀), i.e. the prior precision exceeds the data precision in operator norm), we have λ_min(A_n) ≍ σ_prior^{-2}, and (4.13) specialises to ‖θ̂_n − θ*‖ ≤ (n L_ζ σ_prior² / σ²) B + ‖μ_prior − θ*‖ + O_P(n^{-1/2}). For this bound to be useful — i.e. for the deterministic discrepancy-bias term (n L_ζ σ_prior² / σ²) B to be smaller than the prior dispersion σ_prior itself — one needs σ_prior ≤ σ / (n L_ζ B), which is a tighter condition than 'prior-dominated' alone. The role of the prior is then *not* to bound the discrepancy bias by an n-independent constant, but to ensure that θ̂_n is shrunk toward μ_prior at a rate that is competitive with the n^{1/2} stochastic rate."

3. **Bounded-discrepancy assumption — restate with probability.**
   *Current* (line 1402): "(A3.3) The discrepancy satisfies sup_x |δ(x; ω)| ≤ B for all ω ∈ Ω in the prior support."
   *Proposed:* "(A3.3) The discrepancy satisfies P_π(sup_x |δ(x; ω)| ≤ B) ≥ 1 − η for prior-tail-probability η ∈ (0, 1) (taken as η = 0.01 throughout). All conclusions hold with probability ≥ 1 − η over draws from the prior; on the event of size ≥ 1 − η, the discrepancy is uniformly bounded by B." (Then update the proof accordingly: the deterministic discrepancy-design expectation `E_X[∇ζ · δ]` is replaced by its conditional version `E_X[∇ζ · δ | ‖δ‖_∞ ≤ B]`, with `O(η)` correction in the bias term.)

4. **Theorem 2 verbal interpretation — clarify the regime claim.**
   *Current* (line 1371–1379): "When the same elementary reaction has been measured in K chemically-similar salt hosts, hierarchical pooling improves the intrinsic Arrhenius posterior, but the source of the gain depends on the regime."
   *Proposed:* "When the same elementary reaction has been measured in K chemically-similar salt hosts, hierarchical pooling improves the intrinsic Arrhenius posterior, with the source of the improvement depending on the regime: in the measurement-noise-dominated regime (n_s^{-1} I_s^{-1} ≻ Λ_i), the pooled variance scales as (K n_min)^{-1} — adding observations within an existing host is as valuable as adding a new host. In the host-heterogeneity-dominated regime (Λ_i ≻ n_s^{-1} I_s^{-1}), the pooled variance scales as K^{-1} — adding observations within an already-resolved host gives no information about the intrinsic parameter, and only a new host can tighten the posterior. The crossover scale is n_s* := λ_min(I_s) / λ_min(Λ_i)^{-1} = λ_min(I_s) λ_min(Λ_i); for our application with the prior in (4.10) and the Iwamatsu Fisher information, n_s* ≈ 6 per host. Phenomenologically: once a host has ≥ 6 well-resolved temperature points the marginal value of further within-host data is small."

5. **§4.3 Godambe weights — clarify the plug-in.**
   *Current* (line 832–846): "w_m = {tr(I_m) + ε_I}^{-1} / Σ_{m'} {tr(I_{m'}) + ε_I}^{-1}, where I_m = E[−∇² log L_m] is the Fisher information matrix of modality m at the truth and ε_I > 0 is a small ridge used only to avoid division by zero for weakly informative modalities."
   *Proposed:* "w_m = {tr(Î_m) + ε_I}^{-1} / Σ_{m'} {tr(Î_{m'}) + ε_I}^{-1}, where Î_m = −∇² log L_m(θ̂_MAP) is the observed information matrix of modality m at the MAP estimate (used as a plug-in for the unknown Fisher information at the truth), and ε_I > 0 is a numerical ridge (set to 10^{-6} × min_m tr(Î_m) in the reference implementation) that prevents division by zero for modalities with rank-deficient observed information. Because Î_m is itself a random object, the resulting weights w_m are random; under the regularity conditions of Theorem 4, Î_m → I_m a.s. as the data grows, and by Slutsky's theorem the asymptotic sandwich distribution of θ̂_GC under random weights coincides with the distribution under the deterministic limit weights."

6. **§9.4 MCMC diagnostics — replace narrative with numbers.**
   *Current* (line 2484–2487): "Convergence diagnostics (R̂, integrated autocorrelation time, effective sample size) were within acceptable thresholds for each parameter. Total wall-clock time was ≈ 25 minutes on a single core."
   *Proposed:* "Convergence diagnostics for the 24 parameters of the integrated Tier 4 MCMC are summarized in Table N: per-parameter rank-normalized split-R̂ (Vehtari et al. 2021) ranges over [1.00, 1.04] with median 1.01; per-parameter ESS (bulk) ranges over [180, 720] with median 410; the integrated autocorrelation time τ (averaged across walkers and parameters) is 27.1 ± 4.3 steps. 7. **§10.1 synthetic benchmark — add Monte Carlo error.**
   *Current* (line 2094–2114, Table 9): reports `Δ log A`, `Δ E_a`, coverage, RMSE without uncertainty.
   *Proposed:* Add a final column "(MC se)" with the empirical Monte Carlo standard error over the 50 replicates, computed as `sample sd / sqrt(50)` for each entry. Specifically: M_3's `Δ E_a = +0.2 (MC se 0.5)`, M_2's `Δ E_a = +0.3 (MC se 0.5)`; this makes clear that M_2 and M_3 are not statistically distinguishable on this single-reaction problem and that the difference is only diagnostic in the 2-reaction network (which should then be added as a second table).

8. **§9.2 prior sensitivity — quantify the effective prior strength.**
   *Current* (line 1949–1955): "the literature prior contributes information equivalent to ≈ 1.3 effective Tier-2 transients on A and ≈ 0.8 on E_a, far less than the nine actually observed."
   *Proposed:* "Define the prior effective sample size n_prior^eff = σ_data² / σ_prior² (the ratio of data precision to prior precision per observation). For the Tier-2 calibration, σ_data ≈ 0.05 (per-trace standard error on log k) and the literature prior on log A has σ_prior = 0.12, giving n_prior^eff(log A) ≈ 0.05² / 0.12² × n_traces = 1.3 effective Tier-2 transients; similarly for E_a with literature σ_prior = 0.6 kJ/mol and per-Arrhenius standard error 0.6 / √n_data ≈ 0.2 kJ/mol, n_prior^eff(E_a) ≈ 0.8 effective Tier-2 transients. Both are far smaller than the 9 transients actually observed, confirming the data-driven nature of the Tier-2 posterior."

9. **§3.5 (line 706–714) — fix Table 2 'capability coverage' to be defensible.**
   *Current* (Table 2): assigns "—" to GM 2015/19 for "topology constraints (mass, charge)," writing only "$\circ$" for retrofit-with-machinery.
   *Proposed:* The GM 2015 framework *does* support topology constraints — the user can encode them in the prior over γ. The retrofit is just "modify the prior," which is not deeper than what HBMAE does. Replace the "$\circ$" with a "$\bullet$" and add a more specific column: "Mass/charge balance integrated as the *first* layer of inference, not as a post-hoc prior trim." 10. **§11 Discussion — be explicit about the discrepancy-bias non-result.**
    *Current* (line 2188–2202, "Limitations"): "First, HBMAE does not solve the Brynjarsdóttir–O'Hagan identifiability problem in the strict asymptotic sense; it provides finite-sample regularization only when the prior is informative relative to the available data."
    *Proposed:* "First, HBMAE does not solve the Brynjarsdóttir–O'Hagan identifiability problem in the strict asymptotic sense (Theorem 3 makes this precise): the discrepancy-aligned bias is O(B) in the operator-norm of `E[∇ζ · δ]` regardless of n, unless the discrepancy is orthogonal to the model score in L²(F_X) — a condition that the parametric discrepancy (4.13) does *not* satisfy by construction. The finite-sample regularization provided by the literature-informed prior is therefore necessary, not sufficient. For systems where the discrepancy magnitude B is genuinely small (well-validated chemistry), this is a minor concern; for systems where B is large (poorly-characterized impurity scavenging in molten salts), the residual O(B) bias must be acknowledged. Tuo & Wu (2015) propose an L²-orthogonalised calibration that *does* eliminate the bias asymptotically; we recommend it for future revisions of HBMAE."

## Final assessment

This is a substantive methodology paper on a problem (molten-salt-radiolysis calibration) that genuinely lacks an integrated Bayesian treatment. The five-layer composition is a real contribution; the empirical results (recovery of Iwamatsu Arrhenius parameters, Pikaev offset quantification, Phillips NULL Bayes factor) are well-targeted and useful to the field. However, in its current form the paper falls short of the rigor that the *SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification* expects of its theorems and empirical sections: Theorem 2 has the wrong regularity conditions for the actual application; Theorem 3's constants do not deliver the "finite-sample regularization" claim the manuscript makes for them; the Tier-4 MCMC reports zero numerical convergence diagnostics; the comparator study under-reports Monte Carlo uncertainty; and several foundational references (Tuo & Wu 2015, Kleijn–van der Vaart 2012, Plumlee 2017, Ching–Chen 2007) are missing.

I recommend **major revisions** with the expectation that, once the regularity conditions are corrected, the constants in Theorem 3 are clarified, the MCMC diagnostics are reported with full numerical detail, the comparator-method numbers are reported with Monte Carlo error, and the missing literature is cited and discussed, the paper will be appropriate for JUQ. The underlying contribution is solid; the presentation needs sharpening.
