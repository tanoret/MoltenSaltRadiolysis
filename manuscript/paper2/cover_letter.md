# Cover letter — Paper 2

**To:** Editorial office, *Journal of Nuclear Materials*

**Re:** *A Calibrated Multi-Salt Radiolysis Model for Molten Chloride and Fluoride Reactor Fuels.*

Dear Editor,

Please consider the attached manuscript for publication in *Journal of Nuclear Materials*.

Molten salt reactors (MSRs) require quantitative predictions of cover-gas Cl₂ and F₂ inventories under sustained radiation. The pulse-radiolysis and chronic-irradiation data needed to constrain these predictions are scattered across forty years of literature, multiple facilities, multiple host salts, and an array of observation modalities (transients, scalar rates, Arrhenius pairs, censored detection bounds, G-values, and molar absorptivities). No published model previously assembled these data into a single calibrated kernel suitable for screening-level reactor predictions.

The manuscript assembles thirteen digitized literature sources (Cr, Zn, Nd, Cf rate constants from Brookhaven LEAF; Pikaev and Hagiwara legacy data; Davis F₂ G-values; Toth–Felker recombination kinetics; Makarov X₂⁻ molar absorptivities; the Phillips NaCl-UCl₃ chronic-irradiation non-detection benchmark) into a 73-observation multi-modality corpus, calibrates a chloride and a fluoride radiolysis kernel against it with the HBMAE framework of the companion methodology paper, and applies the calibrated model to two operational predictions: cover-gas Cl₂ inventory in a NaCl-UCl₃ MCFR over 60 years (a Phillips-bounded screening prediction with the U(III)/U(IV) buffer assumption), and the steady-state F₂ inventory in FLiBe-UF₄ at operational temperature (within a factor of three of a notional 100 Pa screening threshold).

The manuscript is honest about the limits of the predictions: the MCFR margin is dominated by the assumed U-redox sink, and a sensitivity analysis (§8.1.4) shows the margin collapses if the buffer is incomplete; the chemistry omitted from §3 (oxide impurities, redox-active fission products) is enumerated. The chemistry-feature regression is documented as descriptive, not predictive, for elements not yet measured.

The companion methodology paper, *Hierarchical Bayesian Mechanism-Adequacy Estimation* (submitted to *SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification*), develops the framework; the present manuscript stands alone for *Journal of Nuclear Materials* readers and includes a one-page methodology summary in §1.4.

We have no conflicts of interest to declare. The manuscript has not been submitted elsewhere.
